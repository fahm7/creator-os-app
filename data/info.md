Creator OS
01 Observation
A creator records a forty minute conversation on Friday. By Sunday it is a thread, three clips, a
newsletter section and two carousels, each drafted in under a minute. She still lost Sunday to it,
rewriting sentences that were fluent and wrong about her, and cutting an angle she had
published in March.
The pattern is measured. In a pre-registered experiment with 758 Boston Consulting Group
consultants, tasks inside the model's capability were done faster and rated higher; on a task
chosen to sit outside it, consultants using the model were about 19 percentage points less likely
to reach the right answer than consultants using nothing at all. The two kinds of tasks did not
look different. Confident help in the wrong place cost more than no help.
A second study names the damage here. Writers given story ideas by a language model produced
work rated more creative and better written, and those stories were more similar to one another
than stories written without it. Individually better, collectively flatter.
So the comfortable reading, that drafting is solved and only taste is left, is too coarse. Some taste
work is checkable in seconds and by a machine: is this claim in the transcript, has she said it
before. Some cannot be checked until after publication. The repetitive work and the delegable
work are two different sets, and the boundary runs through the middle of the workflow.
THE HYPOTHESIS
If a system treats the creator's published archive as a standard that outputs must pass rather
than as context they are generated from, then the share of drafts accepted without
substantive rewriting rises and holds across runs, because the judgment that costs the
creator most attention (does this sound like me, have I said it already) becomes a check the
system runs first.
02 Your Challenge
Your user is one real creator or small content team, not a persona. They have at least twenty
published pieces you can read, you can reach them weekly, and they personally accept or reject
every output. If you cannot name them, you do not yet have this capstone.
Take their raw material, in whatever form it arrives, and carry it to something publishable on a
channel they already use. One workflow run many times beats four run once. Their rejections
are data: a draft rejected and fixed in two minutes is a different result from one rejected and
abandoned. Everything about how is yours; those are the decisions being assessed.

03 What We Already Know
The repetitive work and the delegable work are two different sets
Reformatting a paragraph for a channel and choosing which claim to lead with both feel like
writing, cost similar effort, and sit on opposite sides of the line.
Question: Where does that line sit in your creator's workflow, and what did you observe to find
it rather than guess?
The model pulls every creator towards the same middle
Assistance raised individual quality and reduced variety across writers at once. A system that
improves the average draft while eroding what makes this creator recognisable has taken
something while appearing to help.
Question: What would you measure to catch that drift, and against what reference?
Two judgments in this workflow look alike and are not
Whether a claim appears in the source can be checked against a document, in seconds, without
the creator. Whether the piece deserves to exist cannot be checked before it ships.
Question: Which of your checks can run before the creator's attention is spent, and which
cannot run at all?
04 Questions to Think About
1. What did you notice about your creator's week that they did not tell you?
2. Which part would you still do by hand if the model were twice as good?
3. Why should a model decide this rather than a rule you could write down?
4. How would your creator notice the system had drifted from their voice?
5. What must your system remember, and how would you prove it mattered?
6. What happens if you remove the human? If you remove the model?
7. Publishing cannot be undone and takes one click: what stands before it?

05 How We Will Evaluate It
Criterion What we are looking for
Problem understanding A real creator, and friction they actually feel.
Placement of agency Each decision sits where the cost of error puts it.
Evidence of acceptance Measured across repeated runs, not shown once.
Traceability Every claim walks back to the source it came from.
Product quality The slice runs end to end, including when things fail.
Learning Evidence changed your design, and you show where.
COMPLEXITY IS NOT CREDIT
More agents, models, tools and features do not make a stronger capstone. Choosing not to
use a technique, and defending the choice, counts as a decision.
